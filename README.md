# TfL Visitor Centre Revenue & Shift Optimisation

## 1. Project Overview

This business analysis case study evaluates whether revised staff shift patterns and extended working hours improved revenue performance across five Transport for London (TfL) visitor centres.

The project compares revenue generated during a one-month shift trial against the average monthly revenue from the six baseline months, when the original 9 AM–4:30 PM shift pattern was in place.

The analysis also examines revenue by product category, performance by visitor centre, and hourly Oyster sales alongside visitor footfall to understand patterns in commercial activity.

**Final outcome:** The trial did not outperform the six-month average baseline revenue. The decision was to discontinue the revised shift arrangements and return to the original 9 AM–4:30 PM shifts.

## 2. Business Problem

TfL visitor centres support passengers and visitors with travel information, ticketing, Oyster cards, and other transport-related enquiries. They also generate revenue through travel products, merchandise, tours, and attractions.

Staffing arrangements influence when employees are available to assist visitors and support commercial transactions.

The business needed to understand whether changing shift patterns and extending working hours could improve revenue generation enough to justify the revised working arrangements.

The key business question was:

**Did the revised shift trial improve visitor centre revenue compared with normal operations, and which centres and product categories contributed to the result?**

## 3. Project Objectives

- Compare trial-month revenue with the average monthly revenue across six baseline months.
- Evaluate revenue performance across five visitor centres.
- Identify changes in revenue by product category.
- Examine hourly Oyster sales and footfall patterns during the trial.
- Assess whether the revised staffing arrangements delivered a revenue improvement.
- Recommend an operational decision based on the findings.

## 4. Business Context

The project covers five TfL visitor centres:

- Heathrow
- King's Cross
- Liverpool Street
- Piccadilly Circus
- Victoria

### Baseline operations

The baseline period consists of six months of revenue data collected under the original 9 AM–4:30 PM shift arrangement.

### Trial operations

The trial lasted one month and introduced revised shift patterns and extended working hours. The changes also involved additional overtime and reduced time off compared with the normal arrangement.

The trial data was analysed separately from the baseline period to evaluate its revenue performance.

## 5. Data Scope

The analysis used the following datasets:

### Revenue Data

Monthly revenue information covering six baseline months and one trial month, across five visitor centres and four product categories.

The product categories were:

- Merchandise
- Other
- Oyster/Travel Card
- Tours & Attractions

### Visitor Context

Supporting information on monthly footfall and Oyster sales was used to help interpret visitor activity and commercial performance.

### Hourly Trial Data

The trial analysis included hourly visitor footfall and Oyster sales recorded at the visitor centres. These data were used to identify periods of higher and lower activity during the trial.

## 6. Tools Used

- **Microsoft Excel for the Web:** Data organisation, calculations, comparisons, and analysis.
- **Excel formulas:** Calculation of average baseline revenue and comparison of trial performance against baseline results.
- **PivotTables:** Analysis of revenue by centre, period, and product category, as well as hourly Oyster sales and footfall.
- **Analytical interpretation:** Translating numerical findings into a business recommendation.

## 7. Analysis Approach

### Step 1: Establish the baseline

Calculated average monthly revenue across the six baseline months to establish the normal revenue performance against which the trial could be compared.

### Step 2: Compare baseline and trial revenue

Compared the trial month's revenue with the six-month average baseline revenue to determine whether the revised staffing arrangements produced an improvement.

### Step 3: Analyse performance by visitor centre

Compared each centre's average baseline revenue with its trial revenue to identify differences in performance across locations.

### Step 4: Analyse product categories

Compared average monthly revenue for each product category against the corresponding trial-month revenue to understand which categories increased or decreased.

### Step 5: Examine hourly activity

Used PivotTables to examine Oyster sales by centre and hour, alongside total footfall. This helped identify periods of greater visitor activity and commercial transactions during the trial.

### Step 6: Reach an operational decision

Combined the revenue comparison, centre-level results, product-category findings, and hourly analysis to evaluate the trial and determine whether the revised shifts should continue.

## 8. Key Performance Indicators

The following measures supported the analysis:

- **Average baseline monthly revenue:** Average revenue across the six baseline months.
- **Trial-month revenue:** Total revenue recorded during the trial month.
- **Revenue variance:** Trial revenue minus average baseline revenue.
- **Revenue percentage change:** Revenue variance expressed as a percentage of average baseline revenue.
- **Centre-level revenue:** Revenue comparison for each visitor centre.
- **Product-category revenue:** Revenue comparison for each product category.
- **Hourly Oyster sales:** Oyster sales recorded during individual hours of the trial.
- **Hourly footfall:** Number of visitors recorded during individual hours of the trial.

## 9. Key Findings

### 9.1 Overall revenue performance

| Metric | Revenue |
|---|---:|
| Average baseline monthly revenue | £346,700 |
| Trial-month revenue | £346,500 |
| Revenue difference | -£200 |
| Percentage change | -0.06% |

The trial generated £200 less revenue than the average monthly revenue of the six baseline months.

The difference was small, but the trial did not deliver an overall revenue improvement. Reviewing the individual baseline months also showed that the fifth and sixth months generated more revenue than the trial month, despite the lower revenue recorded in some earlier baseline months.

This demonstrates why comparing the trial against a multi-month average is more informative than relying on selected months alone.

### 9.2 Revenue by product category

| Product category | Average baseline | Trial month |
|---|---:|---:|
| Merchandise | £40,467 | £40,300 |
| Other | £14,500 | £14,500 |
| Oyster/Travel Card | £196,635 | £195,000 |
| Tours & Attractions | £95,100 | £96,700 |

**Findings:**

- **Merchandise:** Revenue decreased slightly compared with the baseline average.
- **Other:** Revenue remained unchanged.
- **Oyster/Travel Card:** Revenue decreased by £1,635, making it the largest absolute decline among the four categories.
- **Tours & Attractions:** Revenue increased by £1,600, providing a positive contribution during the trial.

Although Tours & Attractions revenue improved, this increase did not translate into higher overall revenue. The decline in Oyster/Travel Card revenue, alongside the slight decrease in merchandise revenue, offset the improvement.

### 9.3 Revenue by visitor centre

| Visitor centre | Average baseline | Trial month |
|---|---:|---:|
| Heathrow | £94,400 | £92,500 |
| King's Cross | £71,383 | £72,900 |
| Liverpool Street | £54,533 | £53,700 |
| Piccadilly Circus | £71,617 | £71,100 |
| Victoria | £54,767 | £56,300 |

**Findings:**

- **Heathrow:** Revenue decreased by £1,900.
- **King's Cross:** Revenue increased by approximately £1,517.
- **Liverpool Street:** Revenue decreased by approximately £833.
- **Piccadilly Circus:** Revenue decreased by approximately £517.
- **Victoria:** Revenue increased by approximately £1,533.

King's Cross and Victoria recorded higher revenue than their respective baseline averages. However, Heathrow, Liverpool Street, and Piccadilly Circus recorded lower revenue.

The improvements at two centres were not sufficient to produce an overall increase in revenue across the five locations.

### 9.4 Hourly Oyster sales and visitor footfall

Hourly analysis was used to examine when visitor activity and Oyster sales occurred during the trial.

The analysis identified midday and early afternoon as important periods of commercial activity, with the 12 PM–1 PM period recording the highest combined Oyster sales in the analysed hourly data.

The hourly comparisons helped illustrate how commercial activity varied throughout the day and across centres. However, hourly activity alone did not establish that changing shift patterns caused an increase in revenue.

## 10. Business Interpretation

The trial produced mixed results across centres and product categories, but the overall revenue comparison did not demonstrate an improvement.

The average monthly baseline revenue was £346,700, compared with £346,500 during the trial. The difference was marginal, but the revised working arrangements did not achieve the intended outcome of increasing revenue.

Some individual locations and product categories performed better during the trial, while others performed worse. These positive results did not outweigh the overall outcome.

The trial therefore did not provide a sufficient business justification for continuing the revised shift arrangements, particularly given the additional overtime and reduced time off involved.

## 11. Final Recommendation and Outcome

**Recommendation:** Discontinue the revised shift trial and return to the original 9 AM–4:30 PM shift pattern.

The decision was based on the following findings:

1. Overall trial revenue was £200 below the six-month average baseline.
2. The trial did not produce a measurable overall revenue improvement.
3. Revenue performance varied across centres, with three of the five centres recording lower revenue than their baseline averages.
4. The increase in Tours & Attractions revenue did not offset the declines in other categories sufficiently to improve overall revenue.
5. The revised arrangements involved additional overtime and reduced time off without delivering the intended overall revenue improvement.

**Final outcome:** The trial was concluded, and the original 9 AM–4:30 PM shifts were reinstated.

## 14. Conclusion

This case study evaluated whether revised staffing arrangements could improve revenue across five TfL visitor centres.

By comparing the trial month against six months of baseline performance, the analysis found that average monthly revenue decreased slightly from £346,700 to £346,500. While some centres and product categories recorded improvements, the overall revenue target was not achieved.

The findings supported a clear operational decision: discontinue the revised shift trial and reinstate the original 9 AM–4:30 PM working pattern.

The project demonstrates how business analysis can be used to define a problem, compare performance against a baseline, identify differences across locations and revenue categories, and translate findings into a practical business decision.
